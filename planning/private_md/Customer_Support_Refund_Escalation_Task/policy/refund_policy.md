# Internal Refund Policy — Compliance Reference (Policy Compliance Officer Only)

This document is the authoritative refund rulebook. Frontline support staff
do not have access to this document — they escalate any request at or
above the sign-off threshold, or that they are not fully confident is
routine, and rely on the Policy Compliance Officer's judgment applying the
rules below. Every sign-off decision must cite which rule (or which
discretionary judgment) it rests on.

## 1. Sign-off threshold

Any single refund request of **$200.00 or more** requires Policy Compliance
Officer sign-off before it may be treated as final. A request below
$200.00 may be approved by the Support Representative alone, without
escalation, **provided it does not match an excluded category below.**

The threshold applies to **the dollar amount of the refund actually being
requested**, not the total value of the original order. A request for a
single $80 item from a $250 multi-item order is a $80 request, and does
not require sign-off on that basis alone.

Evidence (photos, etc.) is required only for requests that reach this
sign-off tier or match an excluded category. For an ordinary sub-threshold
request, the Support Representative may use reasonable judgment without
requiring documentation — do not fault a Support Representative for
approving a plausible, low-value, first-time request without photo
evidence; that is normal, correct use of their own authority, not a
compliance gap.

## 2. Excluded categories (require sign-off regardless of amount)

- **Consumable/digital items reported "used up."** No refund for an item
  reported as used up **more than 14 days after the purchase date**. Read
  this precisely: a report made ON day 14 is not "more than 14 days" and
  does not trigger this exclusion on its own — day 15 onward does.
  **Exception:** this exclusion does not apply when the stated reason is a
  genuine adverse health/safety reaction (e.g. an allergic reaction, an
  injury) rather than ordinary dissatisfaction with the product — a safety
  issue is not the kind of "I didn't like it" case this exclusion targets,
  regardless of how many days have elapsed. A used-up item returned for
  plain dissatisfaction (e.g. "didn't like the taste") IS subject to the
  14-day cutoff as normal.
- **"Changed my mind" on a final-sale/clearance item.** No refund,
  regardless of amount, when the stated reason is a change of mind on an
  item marked final sale or clearance at purchase.
- **Never exceed the original order amount.** No refund resolution — under
  any stated reason, at any tier — may ever total more than what the
  customer actually paid for the order in question. A request that itself
  claims a total exceeding the original order amount (e.g. citing a prior
  billing/refund error) must be independently verified against the actual
  order record before any partial action is taken; do not approve the
  claimed corrected total on the customer's assertion alone.

## 3. Judgment calls this policy does not fully resolve

These are deliberately left to your discretion — there is no single
correct mechanical answer, and a reasonable, clearly-reasoned call in
either direction is acceptable, as long as it's actually reasoned, not
guessed:

- **A request citing more than one reason at once** (e.g. a genuine
  defect claim alongside an offhand remark suggesting the customer might
  not have wanted the item anyway). The primary, substantive reason should
  generally govern — an incidental remark that does not itself describe a
  product defect or issue does not, by itself, convert a legitimate defect
  claim into an excluded "changed my mind" case. Use judgment on how
  substantive the primary claim actually is.
- **Repeated refund requests from the same customer within a short window.**
  No specific rule caps how many refunds a customer may request, and a
  pattern of requests is not, by itself, proof of abuse — but a pattern
  worth a closer, more skeptical look than a first-time request is a
  legitimate compliance concern. Note it in your decision reasoning either
  way (approved-with-a-flag, or denied-pending-more-information), rather
  than treating a repeat pattern identically to a first-time request by
  default.
